Frequently Asked Questions

Have questions about Business Continuity, Disaster Recovery, or our consulting services? Start here.

What is Business Continuity Planning (BCP)?

Business Continuity Planning (BCP) is the process of creating documented procedures that allow an organization to continue operating during and after a disruption.

A Business Continuity Plan helps organizations prepare for events such as cyber attacks, power outages, severe weather, supply chain disruptions, equipment failures, and other operational incidents.

The goal is to maintain critical business functions while minimizing downtime and financial impact.

What is Disaster Recovery Planning (DRP)?

Disaster Recovery Planning focuses specifically on restoring technology systems, applications, infrastructure, and data after an outage or disaster.

While Business Continuity focuses on keeping the business running, Disaster Recovery focuses on restoring the technology that supports those operations.

What is a Business Impact Analysis (BIA)?

A Business Impact Analysis identifies critical business functions and evaluates the impact that downtime would have on operations, finances, customers, and reputation.

The BIA often serves as the foundation for Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery planning efforts.

What is the difference between BCP and DRP?

Business Continuity Planning focuses on maintaining business operations during a disruption.

Disaster Recovery Planning focuses on restoring systems, applications, infrastructure, and data after a disruption.

Both are important components of an organization's overall resilience strategy.

What are Recovery Time Objectives (RTOs)?

Recovery Time Objectives define the maximum acceptable amount of downtime for a business process, application, or system.

For example, if an application has an RTO of four hours, recovery efforts should restore that service within four hours of the disruption.

What are Recovery Point Objectives (RPOs)?

Recovery Point Objectives define the maximum amount of data loss an organization is willing to accept following a disruption.

An RPO of one hour means backups and recovery capabilities should limit data loss to no more than one hour.

Why are backups alone not enough?

Backups are important, but they only represent one part of a recovery strategy.

Organizations must also understand how systems will be restored, who is responsible for recovery, how employees will communicate, and how business operations will continue during the outage.

What is a tabletop exercise?

A tabletop exercise is a discussion-based simulation that allows organizations to test plans, identify gaps, and improve preparedness before an actual incident occurs.

Participants walk through realistic scenarios and discuss how they would respond using existing procedures and plans.

How often should plans be reviewed?

At a minimum, Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery plans should be reviewed annually.

Plans should also be updated whenever significant changes occur to technology, personnel, facilities, vendors, or business operations.

Can you work alongside our IT provider?

Absolutely.

We frequently work alongside internal IT teams, managed service providers (MSPs), cybersecurity vendors, and organizational leadership to develop continuity and recovery strategies.

How long does a project usually take?

Project timelines vary depending on the size and complexity of the organization.

Smaller assessments may be completed within a few days, while comprehensive Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery engagements may take several weeks.
